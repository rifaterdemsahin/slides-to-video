🧠 Mental model first

🧠 It is a document, not an editor

This is the single idea that makes Google Vids click. It is not a video editor that happens to live in the cloud — it is a structured document, like a Doc or a Sheet, in which every block happens to be a scene in a video.

  • 📍 It has its own URL type. Vids files live at docs.google.com/videos/<id>. They are not opened from Drive's video player, and they are not Slides files.
  • 🎞️ The scene timeline is the document body. Where a Doc has paragraphs, Vids has scenes. Reordering scenes is reordering paragraphs — that is why the editing feels nothing like Premiere or Resolve.
  • 🧰 The media tray is the toolbar. Where a Doc has bold and italics, Vids has voiceover, music, stock footage and captions.
  • 📤 The output is a real file. Export produces a normal MP4 — H.264, 1920×1080, 30 fps — which you can commit, self-host and publish anywhere.
  • 🔴🔵 This project is the proof. The video on the home page was made exactly this way: a deck imported as ten scenes, narrated, scored, captioned, exported.

🔑 Before you start

🔑 Getting access

Google Vids is not a free standalone product. It ships inside paid Google Workspace, priced per user per month — so the prerequisite is a seat, not a download.

💳 Verified pricing anchor: UK £11.80/user/month list, excluding tax, for the entry business tier at time of capture, with a 50% promotional rate (£5.90) running for the first three months. Prices vary by region, billing period and promotion — always confirm on the live pricing page rather than trusting a figure from any guide, including this one.
  • ✅ What you need: a Google Workspace account on a tier that includes Vids, signed into the same browser profile you'll use.
  • ✅ What you do not need: any desktop software. No download, no install, no GPU, no render farm.
  • 🚫 What will not work: a plain free @gmail.com account expecting the full Vids feature set. Personal-account access is narrower than a Workspace seat — if a feature in the walkthrough below is missing, check the seat before assuming the guide is wrong.

🧭 The walkthrough

🧭 Step-by-step: first video to export

In order. Do not skip step 2 — the ratio decided there is the frame you are stuck with for the rest of the project.

🧠 Write the script somewhere else

Do not open Vids first. Dictate or draft the argument in Gemini and ask for it in presentation form — title, subtitle, three or four bolded points per slide. Vids is an assembly tool; the thinking happens before it.

🎨 If you have a deck, fix the ratio now

Vids imports Google Slides natively, and the deck's aspect ratio becomes the video frame. Set the deck to 16:9 before importing. A 4:3 deck imported into Vids gives you a 4:3 video, and there is no honest fix afterwards.

📄 Create the video document

From Google Drive or the Workspace app launcher, create a new Google Vids file. You get a blank timeline with a playhead and a media tray — the same layout as the first frame of any Doc.

Choose one of three starts: blank, a template, or import from Slides. For the project on this site it was the third.

🎞️ Build the scenes

Each slide you import lands as a scene with its own slot on the timeline. Reorder with the Scene menu; show or hide per-scene duration with Show timing. The readout at the top reads like 00:00.0 / 02:10.8 — playhead position over total runtime.

Treat the scene list as your outline. Ten scenes is a two-minute video; forty is a documentary nobody asked for.

🖼️ Fill the media tray

With the scenes in place, add the non-generated media: Uploads for your own files, Stock for licensed footage and images, Image for graphics, Record for a webcam or screen take.

Work in this order deliberately — structure first, decoration second. Adding stock video before the outline is settled is how twenty minutes of work gets thrown away.

🎙️ Add the voiceover

Every scene carries its own narrator line. You can type the script and let Vids generate the voice, record your own, or upload audio. In this project all ten scenes were AI-narrated — which is why the file has a clean AAC stereo track with no room noise.

Write for the ear, not the eye: shorter sentences than you would put on a slide.

🎵 Score it, then balance

Drop a bed from the Music library under the narration. Then run the audio cleanup — Improve sound quality and Balance sound — which levels speech against music across the whole video in one pass.

This is the step most first-timers skip, and it is the one that makes the difference between "AI made this" and "someone made this".

💬 Turn captions on

Tap Captions in the tray. Burned-in captions mean the video works on mute, in a noisy office, and in every feed that autoplays silently. Free legibility, one toggle.

🪄 Revise with a sentence, not a timeline

Reimagine your video takes a plain-language instruction — "describe any changes that you want to make, from a tiny tweak to a total transformation" — and re-cuts the video. Use it to tighten pacing or change tone rather than dragging clips frame by frame.

👀 Review, then export

Play it end to end once, at 1×. Watch for a slide that outlives its sentence and a narrator line that arrives late. Then export as MP4 — 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC stereo.

📤 Publish the file, not a link

You now own a plain MP4. Commit it to a repo, serve it from GitHub Pages with a <video> tag, and paste the same content into a community as a lesson. Because the export is an ordinary file, you are not locked into sharing a Workspace URL that requires the viewer to have access.

⚠️ Gotchas

⚠️ What goes wrong, and how to avoid it

  • 📐 Wrong ratio, unfixable frame. The deck's aspect ratio becomes the video's. Choose 16:9 before importing, not after.
  • 🔇 Not balancing audio. Generated narration over a music bed without a balance pass sounds like a demo. Balance sound is one click and it is the difference.
  • 💬 Captions off. Most of your audience meets this video muted. Leaving captions off halves the people who can follow it.
  • 🎬 Too many scenes. Slide count becomes runtime. Ten scenes ≈ two minutes; the format punishes padding because the viewer cannot skim.
  • 🔗 Sharing the doc instead of the export. A Vids URL needs the viewer to have Workspace access. Export the MP4 and self-host it — that is the point of a document type with a file output.
  • 💳 Assuming it is free. Vids comes with a paid Workspace seat. Budget the seat, not the editor.
  • 🪄 Over-relying on Reimagine. It is excellent for pacing and tone. It is not a substitute for having decided what the video argues.
✅ The 60-second version: write the script in Gemini → set the deck to 16:9 → create a Vids doc → import the deck → AI voiceover per scene → music and Balance sound → captions on → export MP4 → publish the file.
